# 2026 Congressional App Challenge (CAC) Rules

## 1. Eligibility

### Age

* Students must be enrolled in middle school or high school on **October 26, 2026**.
* Middle school varies by region but typically includes **grades 6–8**.

### Location

* Students may only compete in a Congressional District that is hosting an App Challenge.
* Participants may compete in either:

  * The district where they **live**, or
  * The district where they **attend school**.
* Students may submit an app in **only one district** and cannot compete in multiple districts.

### Teams

* Students may compete individually or in teams of **up to four (4) students**.
* Teams with more than four students are not eligible.
* At least **half of the team members** must live or attend school in the same Congressional District.

### Prior Projects

* Students may submit any app created **after October 30, 2025**.
* If submitting version 2.0 of an app, only the **new coding** should be highlighted for consideration.

### One Entry Per Person Per Year

* Students may submit **only one app per year**.
* Multiple entries across different teams are not permitted.

---

# 2. Online Registration

### Individual Registration

Students need:

* A personal email address (not a school email account)
* Home address, including:

  * 9-digit ZIP Code
  * Congressional District
  * Member of Congress
* School address, including:

  * 9-digit ZIP Code
  * Congressional District
  * Member of Congress
* Parent or guardian's name and email address
* Coding teacher or mentor's name and email address (optional)

### Team Registration

Teams will need:

* All of the information listed above for **each team member**
* One team may participate **once** as a group

---

# 3. App Parameters

### Topic

* The app may be about **any topic**.

### Platform

* Apps may be developed for any platform, including but not limited to:

  * Web apps
  * Desktop/PC apps
  * Mobile apps
  * Browser extensions
  * Robots
  * Augmented Reality (AR)
  * Virtual Reality (VR)
  * Wearable devices
  * Smart devices
  * Embedded systems

### Programming Language

Participants may use any programming language, including but not limited to:

* C
* C++
* Objective-C
* C#
* Java
* JavaScript
* Python
* Ruby
* PHP
* Swift
* Block-based coding languages

### Functionality

* The app must demonstrate a meaningful level of functionality to be considered competitive.

### Content Suitability

The app must:

* Adhere to standards of decency and respect.
* Not be indecent, defamatory, offensive, or in poor taste.
* Not promote hatred, violence, or threaten any community.
* Not contain pornography, obscenity, or sexual activity.
* Not violate intellectual property rights, privacy rights, or other legal rights of third parties.

### Originality

* The app must be original and created solely by the contestant(s).
* All coding and technical development must be completed by the student or student team.
* Open-source libraries, frameworks, and external tools may be used if they are clearly documented.
* The project must demonstrate significant personal effort and technical understanding.
* No other party may claim ownership or rights to the app.

### AI Usage

* AI tools are permitted.
* All AI usage must be fully disclosed in the submission materials.
* AI may support portions of development but **cannot replace the student's own technical work**.
* Participants must demonstrate significant individual contributions and understanding of the app.

# 4. Online Submission

Consult the **Submission Checklist** for guidance. After the submission period ends, submissions **cannot be modified**.

Students can register for the 2026 Congressional App Challenge at **congressionalappchallenge.us**.

For team submissions:

* One teammate creates the team profile.
* That teammate may invite up to three additional members.
* Every student completes a personal profile and eligibility quiz.
* Eligible teams receive an email allowing them to continue the application.

The application includes several questions about the app. The most important component is the demonstration video.

## Demonstration Video (Maximum 3 Minutes)

The video must include:

* Names of all participants
* Name of the app
* A clear one-sentence explanation of the app's purpose
* The target audience
* Programming languages and development tools used
* A demonstration of the app's functionality

Additional requirements:

* Length: **1–3 minutes**
* Videos exceeding the time limit may be penalized.
* This is **not** a video-production competition.
* The goal is to clearly explain and demonstrate the app.
* Upload the video to **YouTube or Vimeo**.
* The video must be set to **Public**.

## Application Questions

Applicants should expect questions such as:

* What is the title of your app?
* What is the purpose of your app?
* What inspired you to create it?
* What technical or coding challenge did you face, and how did you solve it?
* What did you learn while participating in the Congressional App Challenge?
* What would you improve in a future version (2.0)?

**Monday, October 26, 2026**

# 6. Exit Questionnaire

After the deadline, students will receive a link to complete an Exit Questionnaire.

For teams:

* **Every team member** must complete the questionnaire individually.
* Team members may use the same link but must submit separate responses.

---

# 7. Winner Selection

Submissions will be reviewed by the Member of Congress and/or their office. A panel of judges may also be used.

Apps may be judged on:

1. Quality of the idea (creativity and originality)
2. Implementation (user experience and design)
3. Coding and programming excellence

Additional rules:

* Judges may request access to the app and source code.
* Failure to provide access may result in immediate disqualification.
* Members of Congress may modify judging procedures or criteria at their discretion.
* Odds of winning depend on both the number and quality of eligible submissions.

---

# 8. Winner Announcements

* Winners are announced by the hosting Member of Congress.
* Results will not be released before the embargo date of **January 15, 2027**.
* Questions about results should be directed to the hosting Member of Congress.

# 9. Prizes

District winners receive:

* Recognition on the **House.gov** website.

* Eligibility for a copyright fee waiver through the **ARTS Act**.
* Additional prizes that may be distributed by the Internet Education Foundation.
* Any district-specific awards offered by the Member of Congress, such as certificates, folders, or commemorative items.
